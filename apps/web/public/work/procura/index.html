CASE FILE 02 · WEEKLY PRODUCTION USE

Procura

Production system Product, supplier, and purchasing information turned into a connected workflow for better decisions — a single-binary Go system handling inventory, suppliers, purchase orders, RFQs, planning, and reporting.

Procurement, reduced to one binary. A single Go binary over SQLite carrying a clinic's entire purchasing spine — reorder points, POs, RFQs, supplier scorecards. No subscription, no cloud mascot.

Why it exists

A clinic's purchasing spine — what's below reorder, what's on order, which supplier actually delivers, what the month cost — split across notebooks, spreadsheets and supplier chat threads drifts apart quietly. Reorder points nobody trusts. POs with no state. Month-end closed by archaeology.

The usual fix rents the clinic its own purchasing data back by subscription. This one runs on a VPS and belongs to the clinic.

What it handles

  • Inventory — 1,142 tracked items with stock levels, reorder points and 23 months of movement history behind each one.
  • Suppliers — 89 of them, each with the purchasing history scorecards are drawn from.
  • Purchasing — POs and RFQs with real states (paid, pending, void), 560 POs deep and counting.
  • Planning — procura plan --week turns the reorder list into the week's draft orders.
  • Reporting — month-end numbers that reconcile, because adjustments were never hidden.
$ procura plan --week → 12 items below ROP · 3 POs drafted $ procura order draft --supplier vetco
sample run — the planner drafts the week's ordersweb app + cli · one binary

Under the hood

  • One Go binary, one SQLite file. The web app and the planner CLI come from the same binary, and the whole ledger is a single file that can be copied, grepped and audited.
  • Adjustments are first-class. Stock movements record receipts, issues and adjustments (adj-in / adj-out) against a monthly closing — so the ledger reconciles instead of silently drifting.
  • Nothing is rented. No subscription, no cloud service in the middle. It runs on a VPS and publishes at procura.wasabietech.com.
  • Agents welcome, read-only. Because the ledger is one SQLite file, my assistant answers “where's the stock” questions with SELECTs against the same numbers. Writes stay with me.

The honest edges

  • Thin on invoices. 560 POs and 109 direct orders raised against 8 invoices recorded — the invoice side is the thinnest part of the spine, and the counts say so.
  • The open month rolls up at close. Movements post in monthly closings, so history stops at the last close while stock levels stay live.
  • No roadmap claims. Everything above is either in the binary or in the database.
